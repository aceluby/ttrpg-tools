# 36 - Paragon's Trial

- Game: Corona Eclipsa
- System: Pathfinder 2e
- Duration: 2.5 hours
- Tone: introspective tension, unstable trust, sea-borne dread

## Clarifying Answers Locked In

- Session length: 2.5 hours
- The session is centered on the voyage aboard `Paragon`
- The tone should be tense and introspective rather than pure action
- `Paragon` should push at the party's emotional boundaries and react to how they answer
- Keeping `Paragon` calm should play as social roleplay inside a skill challenge
- The session needs clear success, failure, and critical failure endings
- The players still do not know the deeper truth about `liveships` or `serpents`
- Small hints about those mysteries are welcome, but the full reveal should wait
- A success means safe arrival and an "in" with the old traders
- A failure still reaches `Vaelport`, but only after a serious serpent attack
- A critical failure throws the party off the ship and cuts the session into island survival with scarce food and
  water
- All four PCs should get pressure and hooks, but `Paragon` is the main star
- Only the PCs and crew are aboard
- The recap should include the recent `Willow Woods` and `Everthrone` material
- The session must feed all four PC threads:
    - `Harlan` and the thieves' guild
    - `Dax` and his father's search for his supposedly dead mother
    - `Jin` and the pull between his monastery and his slavery past
    - `Marcel` and an old fling now in trouble with the guild
- If they reach port, there should be immediate dockside tension between old traders and newer trader interests

## Assumptions

- `Paragon` is traveling toward `Vaelport` on old-trader business rather than carrying major nobles or diplomats.
- The liveship's instability is real but not publicly understood by the crew in draconic terms.
- Liveships do not strictly need crew to function, but `Paragon` prefers having people aboard and `Althea Vestrin`
  prefers not
  to
  sail him alone.
- `Althea Vestrin` has `Paragon` at sea with only a skeleton crew after months of restoration work.
- The party is still early enough in the `Vaelport` arc that strong impressions and leads matter more than answers.
- The voyage should create investigation priorities for next session rather than resolve them.

## Characters

- `Harlan`
    - Spotlight: `Paragon` tests what the `Vaelport` thieves' guild still owns in him and whether he sees himself as
      theirs, the party's, or no one's
    - Useful pressure: the ship hints that old dock hands already know his name and that one guild debt never went
      unpaid
- `Dax`
    - Spotlight: `Paragon` presses on his father, the cave search, and the possibility that `Matilda Wraxor` may not be
      gone in the simple way he believes
    - Useful pressure: the ship has memory-fragments of dragon blood, deep caves, and someone still searching below the
      coast
- `Jin`
    - Spotlight: `Paragon` pries at the fracture between spiritual restraint and the fact that the southern trade still
      rests on chains and silence
    - Useful pressure: the ship suggests someone respectable near `Vaelport` knows exactly how slave routes were hidden
- `Marcel`
    - Spotlight: `Paragon` senses the softest place in him and tests how he reacts to an old lover being in real danger
    - Useful pressure: the ship implies the guild has leverage over her and that she may have tried to send word first
- `Paragon`
    - Spotlight: a liveship carrying two competing dragon souls, one calmer and one absolutely deranged
    - Useful pressure: the more the party lies, lashes out, or retreats, the more the violent mind takes the hull

## Strong Start

- Recap:
    - Last we left off, Corona Eclipsa.... survived the aftermath of dragons and grief, passed through the relief of
      `Willow Woods`, reclaimed silver revealed to be pure `weave`, witnessed `Serel` crowned in a changed
      `Everthrone`, and set their sights south as old debts and older mysteries began to pull harder.
    - Now the party has boarded the `Liveship Paragon` for the passage to `Vaelport`, where `Harlan`'s old guild,
      `Dax`'s family trail, `Jin`'s southern wounds, and `Marcel`'s unfinished romance are all waiting in one harbor.
- Opening image:
    - The sea is almost too calm, especially for how fast you are travelling. The wind shouldn't be moving you this fast
      and you definitely haven't noticed a crew around.
- Immediate problem (after introductions):
    - `Paragon` begins speaking with contradictory moods, probing the party one by one. If they cannot steady the ship,
      something below the waves will answer its distress.

## Scenes

- Introductions with `Althea Vestrin`
    - `Althea Vestrin` looks to the group "It's unsettling the first time, but you get used to it"
    - "The liveship!  I assume this is your first time?"
    - "I'm sorry, I don't think I've formally introduced myself yet, I'm Captain Althea Vestrin"
    - "My first mate over there" - description - "his name is Marek Flint, and the kid" - points + description - "his
      name is Dane"

- First night aboard `Paragon`.
    - Read-aloud:
        - The ship does not creak like timber settling. It breathes in long uneven sighs through the hull, and when the
          lanterns sway you could almost swear the shadows move a moment before the ship does.
    - Hook:
        - The party has to decide whether to introduce themselves like guests, interrogate the ship, test its limits,
          or humor it while the crew pretends this is normal.
    - Scene role:
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
        - meaningful player decision
    - Core content:
        - `Althea Vestrin` introduces herself as captain: brisk, competent, and visibly careful not to upset the ship
        - crew members are afraid of saying the wrong thing near the hull
        - one voice of `Paragon` is patient, lonely, and almost courtly
        - the other is mocking, intrusive, and eager to draw blood with words
        - the ship quickly decides it wants to know what kind of people it is carrying

- Action scene: `Paragon` pries at the party.
    - Read-aloud:
        - A voice comes from the rails in a tone as soft as a confidant. "Tell me the truth," it says, and a second
          voice answers from somewhere below the planks, "No, tell me the wound."
    - Hook:
        - Each PC gets pressed on a personal fault line and must decide whether to answer honestly, dodge, confront the
          ship, or turn the question back on it.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
    - Probe prompts:
        - `Harlan`: "When the guild sees you again, do you plan to kneel, lie, or rob them first?"
        - `Dax`: "If your mother lives below stone and dark water, did she leave, or was she taken from you?"
        - `Jin`: "How many holy men smelled chains and thanked themselves for being powerless?"
        - `Marcel`: "If she sold your name to buy one more night alive, would that make her false or only desperate?"
    - GM note:
        - honest vulnerability, calm boundaries, and compassion toward the ship should cool it
        - contempt, obvious lies, and attempts to dominate without understanding should provoke the unstable soul

- Skill challenge: keep `Paragon` calm.
    - Goal:
        - earn 6 successes before 3 failures
    - Structure:
        - each round, every PC chooses one primary approach
        - use about 3 rounds total unless table energy wants one more exchange
        - repeated tactics become harder unless the fiction changes
    - Good skills:
        - `Diplomacy` to reassure or negotiate with the calmer soul
        - `Intimidation` to establish boundaries with the crueler soul
        - `Performance` to create rhythm, song, or memory that steadies the ship
        - `Occultism` or `Religion` to sense the fracture in the hull's spirit
        - `Nature` to read the sea and notice serpent pressure before it spikes panic
        - `Society` to speak in the language of old-trader respect, duty, and station
    - Difficulty guidance:
        - start at a moderate DC for the party's level
        - raise to a hard DC after clear lies, repeated methods, or needless cruelty
    - Critical success:
        - count 2 successes and let `Paragon` reveal one useful thread cleanly
    - Critical failure:
        - count 2 failures and have the unstable soul lash out at crew, rigging, or course
    - Complications to introduce:
        - a deckhand hears whispering from the bilge and panics
        - the wheel turns on its own toward dangerous water
        - saltwater crawls uphill across the boards
        - someone hears dragon wings overhead where the sky is empty
        - both voices speak through the same plank at once

- Character hook exchanges during the challenge.
    - Read-aloud:
        - The sea goes flat around the hull, as if the world itself is leaning in to hear the answer.
    - Hook:
        - Between checks, let `Paragon` hand each PC one personal lead that will matter once they land.
    - Scene role:
        - important information delivery
        - emotional impact
    - Lead options:
        - `Harlan` hears an old guild phrase or dockside signal the ship should not know
        - `Dax` gets a memory-impression of caves, dragon-song, and a woman tied to loss rather than burial
        - `Jin` hears that chains moved through `Vaelport` under respectable papers and pious silence
        - `Marcel` learns his old fling is in trouble with the guild and may have become leverage in someone else's game

- Success ending: `Paragon` steadies.
    - Read-aloud:
        - The timbers ease under your feet. The ship's next breath is deep, exhausted, and finally whole enough to
          sound tired instead of angry.
    - Hook:
        - The party may ask one final question before landfall, and the calmer soul answers as honestly as it can.
    - Scene role:
        - important information delivery
        - meaningful player decision
    - Reward:
        - `Paragon` offers an old-trader name or berth contact who will hear the party out in `Vaelport`

- Failure ending: serpent attack before port.
    - Read-aloud:
        - The first impact comes from below, hard enough to throw lantern light across the mast. A second later the sea
          splits open around the hull in scales, spray, and teeth.
    - Hook:
        - The party must hold deck, crew, and steering together long enough to force arrival rather than sink into
          chaos.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - things can go wrong
        - action scene
    - The round structure:
        - Stage-setter:
            - `Paragon` loses emotional control and draws a serious serpent attack on approach to the harbor
        - Possible complications:
            - a serpent tangles the rudder
            - rigging snaps and pins a sailor
            - the hostile soul of `Paragon` tries to turn too sharply
            - someone gets dragged toward the rail
            - the calmer soul begs the party not to let the ship kill in panic

- Critical failure ending: thrown into survival.
    - Read-aloud:
        - The hull screams with two voices at once. One begs. One laughs. Then the sea comes up like a wall and the
          whole world becomes salt, darkness, and impact.
    - Hook:
        - Cut immediately to surf, wreckage, thirst, and whoever can still stand on a deserted island.
    - Scene role:
        - hard transition
        - high consequence
    - End state:
        - each PC keeps only the obvious gear they were carrying
        - fresh water and food are immediately scarce
        - `Vaelport` becomes a delayed destination rather than tonight's scene

- Arrival at `Vaelport` if they make port.
    - Read-aloud:
        - `Vaelport` rises through the mist in masts, gulls, curses, and old stone slick with salt. Before the gangplank
          even drops, one dockside argument is already loud enough to turn half the harbor.
    - Hook:
        - The party walks straight into a public clash between an old-trader dock captain and a newer trader ship being
          told they can berth with the other pirates and `Thraxios` scum.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - important information delivery
        - emotional impact
    - Closing value:
        - this should hand the next session multiple live leads and immediate port politics

## Secrets and Clues

- `Paragon` is not one divided mood but two draconic souls competing inside one liveship hull.
- One of those souls fears serpents like kin gone wrong rather than simple sea predators.
- `Vaelport`'s old traders know more about `liveships` than they say in public.
- Someone near the old docks has recent news about `Bernard Wraxor` and the cave search.
- Someone in or around the thieves' guild already knows `Harlan` is coming home.
- Southern slavery in and around `Vaelport` was protected by respectable merchants, forged papers, and willing silence.
- Someone tied to `Jin`'s wider religious world may know more about those routes than they ever admitted.
- `Marcel`'s old fling is in trouble because of guild leverage, not random bad luck.
- The insult about docking with pirates and `Thraxios` scum points to a real fracture between old traders and newer
  harbor powers.
- Whatever waits in the caves tied to `Dax`'s family is being watched by more than one interest.

## Fantastic Locations

- `Paragon`'s night deck.
    - Rendering: lantern-glow on polished rails, long shadows sliding over wet planks, and the wrong sense that the
      ship is studying everyone back.
    - Deeper details: the hull's mood changes the sound of footsteps, ropes tighten themselves when it is angry, and
      the rail is warm in some places and cold in others.
- The whispering bilge.
    - Rendering: black water, beam-shadow like ribs, and soft half-heard voices underneath ordinary slosh.
    - Deeper details: names repeat there that no sailor admits knowing, and one corner always smells faintly of salt,
      blood, and old storms.
- The harbor mouth of `Vaelport`.
    - Rendering: layered masts, shouted berth claims, gulls wheeling over stone piers, and wealth trying not to look
      afraid.
    - Deeper details: old-house colors fly with brittle pride while newer trader ships carry sharper paint, harder men,
      and less patience.
- The disputed dock.
    - Rendering: a narrow berth, a furious old-trader captain, a newer ship refusing insult, and a crowd already
      choosing sides.
    - Deeper details: this is a public test of whose authority matters now, and the wrong first move could make the
      harbor remember the party for all the wrong reasons.

## Important NPCs

- `Paragon`
    - Role: divided liveship carrying the party toward `Vaelport`
    - Motivation: one soul wants understanding and safe passage while the other wants weakness exposed and control
      seized
    - Roleplay trait: shifts from quiet intimacy to vicious ridicule in the middle of a sentence, with pauses that feel
      like another hand taking the wheel
- `Althea Vestrin`
    - Role: captain of `Paragon`, daughter of `Everett Vestrin` of `Vivacia`
    - Motivation: keep `Paragon` sailing because the ship is her only real path to holding command in her own right
    - Roleplay trait: gives orders straight and fast, then walks on eggshells the moment `Paragon` shifts mood
- `Marek Flint`
    - Role: veteran sailor, `Althea Vestrin`'s senior hand, and private tether to the life she walked away from
    - Motivation: keep `Althea Vestrin` alive, keep the ship afloat, and quietly fulfill a promise made to
      `Everett Vestrin`
    - Roleplay trait: watches arguments like weather and steps in only when he is sure the storm is real
- Young deckhand - Dane
    - Role: extra pair of hands aboard a ship that technically does not need many
    - Motivation: stay employed, stay out of `Paragon`'s bad moods, and learn what kind of ship this really is
    - Roleplay trait: tries to look useful before looking brave and checks other people's faces before speaking
- Old-trader dock captain `Mira Fen`
    - Role: harbor traditionalist holding the line at the berth
    - Motivation: keep old-trader authority from being publicly humiliated by newer harbor powers
    - Roleplay trait: plants two fingers on whatever surface is nearest whenever she makes a point and never lowers her
      voice for an insult
- New-trader captain `Soren Vale`
    - Role: rival arrival testing whether the old berth rules still mean anything
    - Motivation: claim equal standing at the docks and refuse inherited humiliation
    - Roleplay trait: smooths his sleeves before every cutting remark and smiles like courtesy is a weapon

## Monsters or Threats

- Main threat: `Paragon`'s emotional spiral
    - Use as:
        - a social and supernatural pressure track rather than combat
    - Effect:
        - every failure worsens sea state, crew fear, and serpent attention

- Failure combat: harbor serpent
    - Level:
        - roughly party level +1
    - Quick PF2e-style profile:
        - AC 27
        - HP 170
        - Fort +21, Ref +17, Will +15
        - Speed 10, Swim 40
        - Jaws +21 for 2d12+10 piercing plus Grab
        - Tail +19 for 2d8+10 bludgeoning with longer reach
    - Abilities:
        - Hull Slam: two actions, damages the ship or knocks creatures prone in a line
        - Drag Overboard: a grabbed target risks being pulled to the rail or into the sea
        - Frenzied By Noise: damage rises while crew panic spreads

- Critical failure threat: greater abyssal serpent
    - Level:
        - roughly party level +3
    - Quick PF2e-style profile:
        - AC 30
        - HP 240
        - Fort +24, Ref +19, Will +18
        - Speed 10, Swim 50
        - Jaws +24 for 3d12+12 piercing plus Improved Grab
        - Crushing Coil +22 for 3d10+12 bludgeoning
    - Abilities:
        - Capsize Surge: wave burst that can throw creatures off balance or overboard
        - Deep Lunge: strikes from below against the rail or clustered targets
        - Terror Wake: fear effect when it breaches beside the ship
    - Encounter note:
        - this is not meant to become a clean tactical win; it is the bridge into island survival

## Treasure / Rewards

- Success rewards:
    - safe arrival with minimal resource loss
    - goodwill or recognition from an old-trader contact tied to `Paragon`
    - one clean first lead in `Vaelport`
- Failure rewards:
    - the party still arrives, but battered, visible, and immediately part of dock gossip
    - surviving the serpent attack can earn rough respect from crew or harbor witnesses
- Information rewards:
    - `Harlan`: a first real pull from the thieves' guild
    - `Dax`: a credible hint that `Matilda Wraxor` may not be simply dead and gone
    - `Jin`: direct proof that southern trade and slavery are knotted together
    - `Marcel`: confirmation that his old fling is a living problem, not a closed chapter

## GM Notes

- `Paragon` should feel like the session's true scene partner, not just transport.
- `Althea Vestrin` works best if she feels genuinely skilled and brave everywhere except in the narrow emotional space
  where
  `Paragon` can take her command away from her.
- Keep the liveship's instability dangerous, but do not explain it too clearly yet.
- For table-facing crew, 3 ship NPCs is enough:
    - `Althea Vestrin`
    - `Marek Flint`
    - one younger deckhand
- Liveships do not need a full mortal crew to function, so you can let `Paragon` feel slightly uncanny by being more
  manned than necessary rather than less.
- If you want the ship to feel real without tracking a full roster, imply one or two other sailors in the background
  only when a scene benefits from it.
- Reward emotional honesty more than cleverness alone.
- The success/failure branches matter, but the main win state is giving `Vaelport` multiple active hooks on arrival.
- If pacing runs short, collapse the middle of the skill challenge into fewer but sharper exchanges and preserve the
  dock scene.
