# 34 - Dragon In The Statue Field

- Game: Corona Eclipsa
- System: Pathfinder 2e
- Duration: 2.5 hours
- Tone: apocalyptic final battle, desperate sacrifice, last-second salvation

## Clarifying Answers Locked In

- Session length: 2.5 hours
- This session is the final battle, not a mixed travel-and-battle session
- The intended feel is near impossible, with victory arriving only in the nick of time
- `Nighteyes` is present for the battle
- `Wilhye Pycey` believes his victory is inevitable and is trying to exterminate the party
- His opening conversation mainly reinforces that `Rhaenard` is cruel and not as clever as he thinks
- If the party talks `Pycey` down, his offer to join him and `Rhaenard` is genuine only toward `Marcel`
- If `Marcel` accepts, `Pycey` still intends to kill everyone else
- `Serel` and `Nyx` begin close to vulnerable `Dorian`
- `Serel` should try to open with a warding spell, but it requires stealth or `Pycey` will counter it
- `Serel` is a full ally with a full stat block
- `Nyx` is a highly dexterous dual-blade assassin, dangerous but not durable
- The statue field's magic-draining property is revealed when `Pycey` unleashes his first devastating spell
- A successful Perception check reveals the weave being absorbed by the girl-on-dragon statue
- A second successful Perception check reveals that the statue is beginning to move
- The party can accelerate the dragon by feeding magic into the rider statue
- Every 2 spell-slot points reduce the countdown by 1 round
- Focus points also count
- `Jin` can pray to `Soryan` for 1 point
- `Dax` can reach toward his assumed dragon patron for 1 point
- `Marcel` can use beast magic to contribute 1 point
- `Dorian` is fully targetable but has partial cover from the dragon he is carving
- `Pycey` should feel like a terrifying boss-tier wizard
- Every activation of the amulet carries the 3% possession chance
- The amulet whispers to `Pycey`, urging him to use more power
- If possession happens, the exact result can be improvised in the moment
- Reinforcements should arrive on rounds 5 or 6 regardless of how well the party is doing
- Those reinforcements are clearly `Rhaenard`'s men
- `Serel` and `Nyx` should not die in this battle
- The ending should be bittersweet:
  - `Dorian` disappears into his dragon
  - the awakened dragons save the group from immediate doom
  - they then fly toward `Everthrone` and the coast
  - they destroy `Red Reaver` ships along the coast
  - `Serel` breaks down while `Nyx` comforts her

## Assumptions

- The previous session ended with confrontation and explanation rather than open battle, so this session can begin on
  a knife-edge conversation and then break immediately into combat.
- The party is still roughly level 7, and the difficulty here is intentionally above normal encounter math because the
  real win condition is survival plus protecting `Dorian`, not clean tactical domination.
- `Pycey`, `the Corterie`, and `Rhaenard`'s soldiers all arrived expecting to end this in one sweep before the statue
  field fully wakes.

## Strong Start

- Recap:
    - Last we left off, Corona Eclipsa.... reached the high mountain shelf where `Dorian` carved a dragon from living
      stone while old statues drank in the excess weave around him. `Serel` stood by him in exhausted devotion, `Nyx`
      kept one eye down every path, and the mountain itself felt like it was waiting for a king, a rider, or a grave.
    - Then `Pycey` and `the Corterie` arrived. Not with pleas for peace, but with the cold certainty of people who
      think history has already chosen its winner.
- Opening image:
    - Wind screams across the statue field. Silver light spills from `Dorian`'s half-finished dragon in pulsing veins
      through the stone. `Pycey` stands with one hand on a glowing amulet at his throat, beard whipping in the gale,
      while old kings and beasts stare down from every side as though they have gathered to watch the wrong ruler claim
      the future.
- Immediate problem:
    - `Pycey` begins with words because he thinks words cost him nothing. If the party engages, he says plainly that he
      does not care who wears the crown. Under `Rhaenard`, he gets control: all the power, none of the blame. If pushed
      hard enough, he offers `Marcel` a place beside him and `Rhaenard`, then signals the kill the instant the offer has
      done all the damage it can.

## Scenes

- Knife-edge parley with `Pycey`.
    - Read-aloud:
        - `Pycey` smiles without warmth and spreads one hand toward the field of kings. "Do you still think this is
          about a better man on a throne? That is peasant logic. `Rhaenard` is cruel, vain, and overproud. Good. Under
          him, I will have control. All the power. None of the blame."
    - Hook:
        - The party can stall, accuse, probe for weakness, try to turn him, or simply take the opening to reposition
          before the killing starts.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
    - Core content:
        - `Pycey` confirms that he serves power, not legitimacy
        - he treats `Rhaenard` as useful rather than admirable
        - if someone nearly reaches him emotionally or politically, he shifts focus to `Marcel`
        - his offer to `Marcel` is sincere in the narrowest and ugliest sense
    - If the party presses toward combat:
        - `Serel` attempts to place a protective ward on `Dorian`
        - require a Stealth check from `Serel` or a covering ally action to hide the casting
        - suggested DC `29` against `Pycey`'s battlefield attention
        - on success, the ward lands before initiative
        - on failure, `Pycey` uses `Counterspell` and opens combat with a sneer

- Action scene: the first spell and the field's secret.
    - Read-aloud:
        - `Pycey` lifts the amulet and the air tears. Silver-violet power crashes across the field with enough force to
          make everyone flinch for death. Then the blast gutters strangely against the statues, its brilliance pouring
          sideways into old stone mouths, claws, and crowns.
    - Hook:
        - The group has one heartbeat to realize the battlefield itself is rewriting the rules of magic.
    - Scene role:
        - important information delivery
        - real potential for things to go wrong
    - Reveal procedure:
        - on `Pycey`'s first large magical effect, roll damage aggressively and say the raw number before applying the
          field reduction
        - then reveal that all magical damage and magical healing in the statue field is reduced by half after normal
          rolls
        - allow immediate Perception checks
    - Suggested Perception thresholds:
        - `DC 24`: the excess weave is being pulled specifically into the statue of the girl riding a dragon
        - `DC 28`: the rider statue subtly shifts, breathes, or tightens around the reins
    - Table note:
        - do not explain this before the spell lands; let the party discover it through fear

- Hold the line at the carving.
    - Read-aloud:
        - `Dorian` braces one hand against the dragon he is shaping, blood and silver magic running together down the
          stone. The half-formed head looms over him like a shield and a tomb both at once.
    - Hook:
        - The party must decide immediately who is screening `Dorian`, who is hunting `Pycey`, and who is feeding the
          rider statue.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - real potential for things to go wrong
    - Core pressure:
        - `Dorian` must stay alive for 6 rounds unless the dragon is accelerated
        - attackers can target him directly, though he has partial cover from the unfinished dragon
        - enemy units should constantly test whether the party can defend a vulnerable objective while under boss-level
          magic
    - Battlefield lanes:
        - central carving shelf where `Dorian` works
        - broken statue rows offering intermittent cover
        - outer archer lanes where reinforcements can appear later
        - the rider statue, close enough to contest but far enough that feeding it costs real actions

- Action scene: awaken the rider statue.
    - Read-aloud:
        - The girl on the dragon looks wrong now, less like sculpture and more like something remembering itself.
          Silver fire pulses inside the stone ribs beneath her.
    - Hook:
        - Any character can spend precious actions and magic to hurry the awakening, but every resource fed into the
          statue is one not spent on survival.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
    - Awakening procedure:
        - track `Dragon Charge` points
        - every 2 points reduce the remaining countdown by 1 round
        - standard spell slots contribute points equal to the spell rank spent
        - focus points contribute 1 point each
        - once per battle, `Jin` can spend 2 actions in prayer to `Soryan` and gain 1 point if his prayer is sincere
        - once per battle, `Dax` can spend 2 actions reaching toward his dragon-linked power and gain 1 point on a
          successful Religion, Nature, or flat faith check at `DC 22`
        - once per battle, `Marcel` can spend 2 actions channeling beast magic and gain 1 point automatically, but it
          visibly marks him with silver witch-light
        - when the countdown reaches 0, the rider-dragon wakes and enters immediately to protect `Dorian`
    - Failure pressure:
        - if the group hoards every resource for offense, the dragon likely arrives too late

- Break the `Corterie`.
    - Read-aloud:
        - `Pycey` does not stand alone. Around him the rest of `the Corterie` spreads with practiced cruelty: one voice
          preparing wards, one pair of hands already shaping a killing route, one quieter mage trying to keep the whole
          machine from collapsing.
    - Hook:
        - The party can try to delete support pieces first, but every second spent away from `Pycey` gives him room to
          escalate.
    - Use each enemy with a clear role:
        - `Elys` keeps allies standing and protects `Pycey` with abjurations
        - `Tiny` controls space, speeds allies, and makes retreat paths miserable
        - `George` body-checks defenders off `Dorian`
    - Soft moral angle:
        - if `Elys` is isolated, frightened, or shown the human cost clearly enough, she may hesitate for a round
        - do not make her easy to redeem in the middle of battle, only wavering

- Fixed-turn reinforcement wave.
    - Read-aloud:
        - Horns answer from below the ridge. Shadows break from the lower slopes in disciplined lines, not rabble. Men
          with `Rhaenard`'s colors hidden under travel grime flood between the statues with bows already raised.
    - Hook:
        - By round 5 or 6, the battlefield gets worse whether the party is winning or not.
    - Reinforcement timing:
        - if the battle feels fast, bring the first squad at the end of round 5
        - if it feels slightly slower, bring them at the top of round 6
        - the arrival is fixed pressure, not a rubber-band correction
    - Reinforcement purpose:
        - collapse safe positions
        - force movement
        - make it obvious that simply grinding down `Pycey` is not the intended win path

- The dragon wakes in the nick of time.
    - Read-aloud:
        - Stone gives way with a sound like a mountain taking breath for the first time in centuries. The rider lowers
          her banner. `Dorian`'s dragon opens its eyes. Every silver line across the battlefield ignites at once.
    - Hook:
        - The awakened dragons do not create a neat victory lap. They create survival, awe, and terrible consequence.
    - End-state target:
        - `Dorian` disappears into his dragon
        - the immediate enemy force is broken, burned, or scattered
        - the dragons then turn toward `Everthrone` and the coast to annihilate `Red Reaver` ships
        - `Serel` collapses in grief
        - `Nyx` is the one who catches her

## Secrets and Clues

- `Pycey` does not respect `Rhaenard`; he plans to rule through him.
- `Rhaenard`'s cruelty makes him easier for `Pycey` to manipulate, not harder.
- The statue field was built to absorb and redirect raw weave rather than simply store it.
- The rider-girl statue is not passive decoration; it is a secondary awakening path.
- The field halves all magical damage and all magical healing because the statues are drinking the spell-energy before
  it fully resolves.
- `Dorian` is no longer only carving a dragon; he is already partly becoming one.
- The amulet `Pycey` uses contains the trapped wizard's essence, and it actively urges him toward overuse.
- Every time `Pycey` channels the amulet, there is a tiny chance he ceases to be the only mind in his own body.
- `Elys` is the least committed member of `the Corterie`, but not harmless.
- `Rhaenard` planned this as extermination, not arrest.
- Even if the party survives, the kingdom after tonight will be shaped by what `Dorian` becomes.

## Fantastic Locations

- The Statue Field
    - Rendering: a high wind-carved shelf crowded with old kings, beasts, and dragons, all veined with stolen silver
      light whenever magic is cast.
    - Deeper details: every spell makes the statues seem more awake, old tool marks hide inside weathered stone, and
      the field feels less like a ruin than a machine waiting for the right sacrifice.
- Dorian's Carving Shelf
    - Rendering: broken chips of white stone, blood on fresh tool lines, and a half-finished dragon rearing over
      `Dorian` like a shield made mid-prayer.
    - Deeper details: the work is too precise for exhausted hands, the dragon's chest already glows from within, and the
      base of the carving creates partial cover with dangerous blind angles around it.
- The Rider Statue
    - Rendering: a girl with banner raised atop a dragon, wind cutting around her so sharply it feels almost staged.
    - Deeper details: the statue drinks overflow magic, tiny shifts appear in the reins and wings after strong spells,
      and up close the face is serene in a way that feels prophetic rather than decorative.
- The Archer Ridges
    - Rendering: broken stone lips and low ridges beyond the main shelf where soldiers can appear already half-hidden.
    - Deeper details: these lanes are perfect for reinforcement fire, retreat becomes difficult once they are occupied,
      and `Nyx` or `Harlan` can use the same lanes for deadly flanking if they seize them first.

## GM Notes

- This session works best if the opening conversation lasts only long enough to sharpen motives and make the first spell
  hit harder.
- The real structure is:
    - survive
    - protect `Dorian`
    - discover the statue rule
    - decide whether to spend magic on survival or acceleration
    - endure reinforcements
    - be rescued by the dragons at the last possible moment
- Let `Pycey` feel unfair in a way that teaches the players the real win condition instead of simply crushing morale.
- Use the half-healing rule aggressively. It should make every recovery spell feel expensive and insufficient.
- If the party is floundering early, let the rider statue show stronger motion after a particularly costly magic
  sacrifice so they understand the alternative path.
- Do not kill `Serel` or `Nyx`.
- If someone would drop `Nyx` or `Serel`, use a near-miss, a last-second intervention, or a hard retreat instead.
- Best closing image:
    - `Serel` on her knees, shaking and sobbing
    - `Nyx` crouched beside her, one hand on her shoulder and blood on both blades
    - overhead, two dragons of silver-lit stone tearing toward the coast while the night below burns
