## Campaign Overview

- `Shenmen` was not always haunted, but it long lived under the threat of oppression from the empire of `Lung Wa`.
- `Lung Wa` installed governors who perpetuated cycles of oppression.
- Local governance in the region became increasingly corrupt.
- After `Lung Wa` collapsed, local leaders became even more corrupt for a short time.
- The ghosts then attacked and killed those leaders, creating a brief reprieve.
- That reprieve did not last, because those leaders returned as monsters and ghosts themselves.
- In the present era, communities live in fear and rely on annual traditions such as `Willowshore`'s `season of ghosts` to make offerings and preserve a peaceful year.
- Act 1 begins in `Willowshore`, an abandoned haunted settlement that remains self-sufficient.
- Most of the campaign takes place in or around `Willowshore`.
- The campaign is primarily about the PCs becoming the saviors of `Willowshore`.
- The four-act story follows a structure inspired by Chinese, Korean, and Japanese narrative traditions.

## Core Hidden Truths

- The people of `Willowshore` are unknowingly repeating the same year over and over again.
- The loop began because the townsfolk were accidentally slain by Governor `Heh Shan-Bao`.
- The apparent starting year is `7108`, but the true present is `7223`.
- `Willowshore` has effectively been reliving the same year for `115` years.
- A later-act twist reveals that the PCs themselves are ghosts who have been living inside the loop.

## The Willowshore Mindscape

- For those trapped inside it, the mindscape is indistinguishable from the living world.
- Everyone within it believes the year is `7108`.
- Day-to-day life functions as though the town were still alive and real.
- Inevitably, the town figures out the truth each year, but the arrival of the new year resets those revelations.
- When the loop restarts, the inhabitants forget and begin again from the same baseline.
- The mindscape has a cyclic time trait and is locked into a repeating yearly loop.
- Everything from the previous cycle is lost when the new loop begins.
- If someone dies during a cycle, that soul enters stasis until the next loop starts.
- Each year proceeds identically unless an outside influence disrupts events.
- Occasionally, manifestations or undead from previous loops appear inside the current cycle.

## Mindscape Inhabitants

- Trapped souls
  - the citizens who died in `7108`
  - they continue existing inside the loop without understanding their true condition

- Non-sapient creatures
  - animal companions and familiars count as trapped souls under the loop's logic
  - other animals and plants otherwise behave much as they would in the normal world

- Monstrous manifestations
  - physical, free-willed monster forms shaped by `Kugaptee`'s influence
  - they cannot be brought back to life
  - they do not become undead after destruction

- Visitors
  - beings who enter the mindscape from beyond its border
  - examples include the shinigami merchant `Shinzo`
  - the `nindoru` fiends encountered in Act 2 also fall into this category

## Locations To Track

- `Shenmen`
  - fictional nation
  - draws on a mix of cultural inspirations
  - strongly shaped by horror traditions

- `Willowshore`
  - haunted, self-sufficient settlement
  - main setting for much of the campaign
  - founded in `7020` during the search for `Tan SuiJing`'s sugi tree after he defeated `Kugaptee`
  - its name comes from "the shore of the willows," meaning "impossible to find"
  - it initially expanded around the construction of a monastery, then grew into a fuller village with inns, farmhouses, and support buildings
  - in `7060` it was conscripted into becoming a major lumber center for `Lung Wa`
  - the lumber bosses dismissed the importance of the founding monastery and eventually targeted the `Tan Sugi` grove
  - that exploitation helps set up the `7062` disaster and the later flood of monsters and ghosts

- `Sze`
  - city ruled by ghostly `Chou Mingxia`

- `Eternal Blaze Ironworks`
  - the forge and metalworking business in `Willowshore`
  - located in `Southbank`
  - responsible for creating metal items
  - currently run by `Yong Wu-Xiu`
  - may be more capable than most townsfolk realize because of `Yong Wu-Xiu`'s training and guarded expertise

- `Willowshore Stables`
  - the town's stable and veterinary center
  - located in `Southbank`
  - run by `Kum Soon-Chong` and his son `Meng-Sung`
  - important because the town relies heavily on cows and other livestock
  - helps explain why most of `Willowshore` does not eat beef

- `Graveside Manners`
  - an old cemetery house turned herbal store
  - located in `Southbank`
  - owned by `So-Jin`
  - carries alchemical elixirs and tools, alchemical reagents, and fresh and dried herbs
  - still contains urns from the original settlers who were cremated to avoid undeath
  - `So-Jin`'s medicinal herbs made that older cremation practice less necessary over time

- `Matsuki Estate`
  - a `Southbank` house belonging to the family of `Matsuki Shou`
  - built by `Matsuki Shou` after he left his apprenticeship under the head carpenter of the `Tan Sugi` monastery
  - the outer walls were strengthened with limestone mixed with blessed salts and holy water
  - those protections help the estate hold against monster invasion

## People Of Willowshore

- The main current topics in town are the recent exodus of the lumber lords, reports that `jorogumo` have taken control of the nation, and preparation for the coming `season of ghosts`.
- Many people define themselves by when their family first settled in town.
- That family history often determines which side of the `Ceiba River` they live on.
- The first wave of settlers mostly lives south of the river and is known as the `Southbankers`.
- Those who arrived after `Lung Wa`'s influence expanded tend to live on the north side in `Northridge`.
- `Northridge` includes workers for lumber bosses, lumber businesses, and imperial guards.
- `Southbankers` are generally more traditionalist and want to preserve their culture through simple life on the land.
- `Northridge` residents are generally more interested in modern pragmatism and urban growth.
- Southerners tend to think `jorogumo` are evil monsters that should be avoided.
- Northerners are more willing to consider `jorogumo` rule over `Shenmen`.
- Even with those tensions, the people of `Willowshore` broadly agree that the town's survival and safety come first.

## Act 1 Chapter 3 Pressure

- `Willowshore Stables` is often one of the first targets of monsters at the beginning of the yearly cycle because of the livestock.
- That pressure commonly leaves fences broken and cattle missing.
- `Kum Soon-Chong` and `Meng-Sung` need those immediate problems resolved before they can turn their attention to other animals.

## Threats And Mysteries

- `Kugaptee`
  - fiend trapped below the roots
  - its influence spreads after the failed attempt to harvest the `Tan Sugi`

- `Night of Broken Blades`
  - the event in `7062` when `Chou Mingxia`'s son attempts to harvest the sacred tree and fails
  - this failure allows `Kugaptee`'s influence to spread further
  - the rush to exploit the `Tan Sugi` grove follows the lumber interests' disregard for the original monastery and sacred protections

- Core mystery structure
  - `Willowshore` is haunted in ways the townsfolk do not fully understand
  - the settlement's yearly cycle conceals a mass-death event and an ongoing loop
  - later revelations shift the campaign from haunted-town survival toward uncovering the truth of the town's condition

## Prep Ideas

- Lean into horror mood, dread, and rituals of communal survival more than encounter brutality.
- Treat `Willowshore` as a living community worth saving, not just a haunted backdrop.
- Keep an eye on what information should remain hidden until later acts so session plans do not accidentally flatten the campaign's major twist.
