# Campaign Dossier

## Current Frame

- Game: `Season of Ghosts`
- System: `Pathfinder 2e`
- Campaign type: published adventure path
- Planner approach: Lazy GM eight-step prep
- Scene approach: flexible scene-to-scene flow, not a rigid script
- Primary setting: `Willowshore` in `Shenmen`
- Campaign shape: four-act story with strong East Asian horror-story inspiration

## Working Campaign Premise

- The campaign centers on `Willowshore`, an isolated self-sufficient settlement living under the yearly fear of the local `season of ghosts`.
- The PCs are meant to become the saviors of `Willowshore`.
- Most of the campaign stays closely tied to `Willowshore`, its people, its haunting, and the pressures gathering around it.

## Tone And Play Priorities

- horror mood matters more than raw encounter difficulty
- dread, mystery, community stakes, and unsettling discoveries should stay foregrounded
- threats can be dangerous, but prep should not assume the main appeal is hard tactical combat
- session prep should support scene flow, revelations, and emotional pressure as much as battles

## Player-Facing Setup

- The campaign begins on the first day of summer in the year `7108`.
- The opening occurs at the beginning of `Willowshore`'s `season of ghosts`.
- There is a free player guide PDF on `paizo.com`, and players should use it when building characters.

## Table Canon

- `Shenmen` has a long history of oppression, corruption, haunting, and fear.
- The people of `Willowshore` maintain annual traditions and offerings meant to preserve a peaceful year.
- `Willowshore` is abandoned in the broader sense, haunted, and still somehow self-sufficient.
- `Willowshore` was founded in `7020` during the search for the sugi tree linked to `Tan SuiJing`.
- The name `Willowshore` comes from "the shore of the willows," meaning "impossible to find."
- The settlement first grew around monastery work, then expanded with inns, farmhouses, and other village structures.
- In `7060`, `Willowshore` was pressed into service as a major `Lung Wa` lumber-harvesting center.
- The demands of the lumber bosses overran the priorities of the founding monastery.
- In `7106`, after `Lung Wa` collapsed, the lumber lords took their wealth and fled.
- `Mago Kai` wants to turn `Willowshore` into a new lumber camp.
- The campaign should treat `Willowshore` as the main practical hub for early and mid-campaign prep.
- The apparent yearly pattern around `Willowshore` can be disrupted by outside influences, and those disruptions matter for session prep.

## Willowshore Social Snapshot

- Current town talk centers on the recent exodus of the lumber lords, news that `jorogumo` have seized control of the nation, and preparations for the approaching `season of ghosts`.
- Many people in `Willowshore` identify themselves by when their families first settled in town.
- Those family histories often map to where people live along the `Ceiba River`.
- The earliest settlers mostly live south of the river and are known as `Southbankers`.
- Families who arrived after `Lung Wa`'s expansion mostly settled on the northern side in `Northridge`.
- `Northridge` includes workers tied to lumber bosses, lumber businesses, and imperial guards.
- `Southbankers` tend to be traditionalists who want to preserve local culture and a way of life rooted in the land.
- `Northridge` residents tend to favor modern pragmatism and urban growth.
- Many southerners view `jorogumo` as evil monsters best avoided.
- Many northerners are more open to the possibility of `jorogumo` ruling `Shenmen`.
- Despite these differences, both sides place `Willowshore`'s survival and safety first when truly pressured.

## Willowshore Locations

- `Eternal Blaze Ironworks`
  - the settlement's metalworking shop
  - located in `Southbank`
  - responsible for producing metal goods for `Willowshore`
  - a likely source for tools, repairs, weapons, armor, and practical town support

- `Willowshore Stables`
  - the town stable and veterinary center
  - located in `Southbank`
  - important to the care of livestock and other working animals
  - especially important in a community that relies heavily on cattle
  - because cattle are so important to the town, most locals do not eat beef

- `Graveside Manners`
  - an old cemetery house converted into an herbal store
  - located in `Southbank`
  - a source for medicinal herbs, alchemical reagents, elixirs, and related tools
  - tied closely to `Willowshore`'s practical health needs and burial customs

- `Matsuki Estate`
  - a `Southbank` house belonging to the family of `Matsuki Shou`
  - reinforced outer walls make it more defensible during monster incursions
