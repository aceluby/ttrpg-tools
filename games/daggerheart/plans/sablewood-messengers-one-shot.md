# Sablewood Messengers One-Shot

- Game: `Daggerheart`
- System: `Daggerheart`
- Format: one-shot
- Source: `Quickstart-Adventure-5-20-2025.pdf`
- Tone: cozy wonder, heroic fantasy, mysterious forest, slight horror

## Clarifying Answers Locked In

- The plan should run the entire quickstart adventure as a single one-shot.
- The epilogue should lightly preserve the sequel hook without weakening the one-shot ending.
- The pre-generated characters should be used as written.
- `Marlowe Fairwind` should be included.
- The table will have `2` PCs.
- The tone should be a mix of cozy wonder, heroic fantasy, mysterious forest, and slight horror.
- The plan should preserve the beginner-teaching beats.
- The session should begin with a strong read-aloud intro that gets the players into the fiction quickly.
- The `5 Room Dungeon` structure should remain implicit rather than explicitly labeled in the scene list.
- The adventure should stay close to the quickstart text and intent.
- The `Thistlefolk` should remain ambiguous rather than being framed as clearly villainous.
- `Hush` and the tavern should be fleshed out enough to support curiosity and slower social play if the players want it.
- `Monsters or Threats` should include full reference material.
- The ending should feel complete even if the `Spirekeeper` hook is never used.

## Assumptions

- This is likely the players' first Daggerheart session, so scene notes should support teaching without slowing
  momentum.
- With only `2` PCs, the GM should treat the printed encounters as flexible baselines rather than fixed quotas.
- The social and exploration scenes should do as much work as the combats in making the one-shot feel full and
  satisfying.
- The Whitefire Arcanist should read as eccentric, capable, and slightly alarming rather than purely whimsical.
- If the party moves quickly, `Hush` and the treehouse can breathe; if the party moves cautiously, the ritual finale
  should still feel like the clear climax.

    - `Garrick Reed`

        - Best use in play: protect the party, steady tense NPC scenes, and hold the line in the finale
    - `Khari Nix`

## Strong Start

- Opening read-aloud:
    - Last light is fading when your carriage finally reaches the `Sablewood`, a forest so old that even the road feels
      like it is trespassing. The trees rise like pillars in a ruined temple, their roots swallowing the trade path in
      long, broken waves. Strange voices drift through the underbrush: the low call of `lark-moths`, the wet croak of
      `lemur-toads`, the skittering rustle of `fox-bats` disturbed by your passing.
    - You ride under orders from `King Emeris` himself. In the carriage behind you sits a large, heavy crate sealed with
      magic and addressed only to the `Whitefire Arcanist` in the village of `Hush`. The reward for delivery is clear.
      The reason for the secrecy is not.
    - The road bends sharply. One wheel lifts for a breathless instant. Then the carriage lurches to a stop.
    - Ahead, an overturned merchant cart blocks the path. Fruit and broken wood litter the trail. Beside it stands a
      creature with the body of a wolf, the face of an owl, and wide folded wings slick with blood. A `strixwolf` mother
      watches you over the corpse of the cart's driver while two cautious pups crowd behind her legs.
    - The forest goes quiet enough that you can hear the horses breathe. Whatever happens next, the `Sablewood` is
      watching.
- Immediate table questions
    - Who is driving the carriage?
    - What is the one unusual thing that character notices about the trees here?
    - What rumor or warning has one of the PCs heard about the `Sablewood`?
- Immediate problem
    - The party must decide whether this is a rescue scene, a threat scene, or the edge of something worse.
- Teaching goal
    - Use the strixwolf interaction for the first action roll and the first clear explanation of `Hope`, `Fear`,
      success, failure, and consequence.

## Scenes

- Scene 1: the merchant cart and the strixwolf mother
    - Read-aloud:
        - The `strixwolf` does not charge. She watches. Her feathers ripple once along her shoulders, and one pup peeks
          around her foreleg with a soft clicking sound. The dead merchant lies half-hidden beyond the cart, one arm
          torn, one hand still clutching the dirt as if he tried to drag himself back to the road.
    - Hook:
        - Ask what the PCs do first: calm the beast, inspect the scene, protect the horses, approach the corpse, or look
          for the real danger.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
    - Teaching beats:
        - Teach the first action roll here, ideally a `Presence` roll to calm or approach the mother.
        - Explain `Hope` and `Fear` as story movement, not just pass/fail seasoning.
        - If the players ask to do something clever instead of the expected approach, follow that instinct and teach
          through their idea.
    - Core information:
        - the merchant is dead
        - the cart has been stripped
        - the strixwolf is a scavenger and protective parent, not the real cause of the killing
        - thorny bramble around the wheel suggests sabotage or capture
        - the merchant's throat was slit
    - Escalation options:
        - the mother calls to something deeper in the trees
        - the pups become exposed and force a moral choice
        - the horses panic
        - the body reveals a clue only once the PCs risk getting closer
    - GM note:
        - Keep the Thistlefolk offscreen but present in the scene's logic from the beginning.

- Scene 2: the road ambush
    - Read-aloud if spotted:
        - A branch snaps in the darkness beyond the trail. Between root-shadow and thorn, small shapes freeze, then
          shift. Eyes catch the light. Blades follow. Whoever set the cart here has realized you are not easy prey.
    - Read-aloud if not spotted:
        - The forest erupts. Thorn-shadowed figures burst from both sides of the trail in a crackle of branches and
          polished stone. The overturned cart was never the problem. It was the door.
    - Hook:
        - Ask who is watching the forest, who is guarding the carriage, and who reacts first when the ambush breaks.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - real potential for things to go wrong
        - important information delivery
    - Stage-setter:
        - `Thistlefolk Ambushers` pin the party down while the `Thistlefolk Thief` tries to take the carriage and its
          sealed delivery.
    - Possible complications:
        - the thief gets hands on the carriage and starts escaping with it
        - a PC is forced away from the crate
        - the strixwolf mother reappears overhead and changes the emotional tenor of the fight
        - one Thistlefolk tries to flee rather than fight to the death
        - the party realizes this is theft under pressure, not necessarily murder for murder's sake
    - Teaching beats:
        - teach the first battle map or abstract combat setup
        - explain PC attack rolls, damage thresholds, armor mitigation, and spotlight flow
        - if useful, teach `Help an Ally` before or during the first hard exchange
    - Two-PC adjustment:
        - Start with `2` Ambushers plus the `Thief` unless the players are cruising.
        - Hold the third Ambusher offscreen as a reinforcement if the scene feels too easy.
        - If the PCs are already stressed from the road scene, let one ambusher spend actions on the carriage rather
          than pure damage pressure.
    - End state:
        - the road is cleared
        - the carriage is still theirs
        - the Thistlefolk remain morally unresolved
        - the montage into `Hush` gives emotional decompression before the village

- Scene 3: arrival in `Hush`
    - Read-aloud:
        - The path eventually bends toward a tall stone pillar carved in ancient dwarven symbols. As the carriage passes
          it, the air gives with a soft pop, as if you stepped through the skin of a bubble. The forest does not vanish,
          but its menace loosens. Friendly voices, lantern-light, and cooking smoke meet you all at once.
    - Hook:
        - Ask what the PCs notice first: the relief, the ward, the people, or the strange normalcy of a village thriving
          inside a haunted forest.
    - Scene role:
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
        - meaningful player decision
    - Core function:
        - let the one-shot breathe
        - shift from danger to curiosity
        - make `Hush` worth protecting before the finale asks the PCs to do exactly that
    - Teaching beats:
        - reinforce that not every scene is a fight
        - model player-authored setting details by asking them to describe local sights, smells, or customs
    - What `Hush` should feel like:
        - cozy, communal, and just strange enough to remind everyone that the forest never truly left
        - a place surviving by custom, warding, and mutual dependence

- Scene 4: finding the `Whitefire Arcanist`
    - Read-aloud:
        - Every friendly answer in `Hush` seems to come with another question, another invitation, or another person who
          knows just a little more than the last. Somewhere between the market chatter and the tavern music, it becomes
          clear that finding the `Whitefire Arcanist` is less about a straight line and more about letting the village
          decide whether it likes you.
    - Hook:
        - Ask who the PCs approach first and what sort of impression they want to make.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - important information delivery
        - emotional impact
    - NPC path options:
        - `Fidget`
            - curious, kinetic child who knows the quickest ways through town
            - good if the players lead with openness, wonder, or humor
        - `Lausa Standworth`
            - sturdy elder carrying a barrel and asking about every weapon by name and make
            - good if the players lead with respect, craftsmanship, or martial pride
        - `Halython Fives`
            - quiet `Clank` with a fox-bat companion and an interest in the Thistlefolk
            - good if the players lead with patience, intellect, or concern about what happened on the road
    - `Clover Tavern` texture
        - six curving stories wrapped around an ancient tree
        - shoes hung on a line at entry, then returned shined and stuffed with tiny gifts
        - music that changes flavor by floor
        - a smell of warm bread, resin, ale, moss, and woodsmoke
        - customs that can be player-authored at the table:
            - a shared toast to the first harvest moss
            - painted stones traded for luck
            - strangers being asked to tell one true thing before being served
    - Optional color scene: `Firstmoss Festival`
        - arm wrestling
        - stone painting
        - trinket stalls
        - villagers talking about the new glowing moss on the crops
    - GM note:
        - Let the players linger here if they want. This scene makes the ritual later matter.
        - The important output is not speed; it is attachment to `Hush` and clean direction to the Arcanist.

- Scene 5: the Arcanist's treehouse
    - Read-aloud:
        - Past the homes and fields of `Hush`, the land opens into groves where fruits and vegetables carry a thin layer
          of glowing blue moss that pulses like a sleeping heartbeat. Beyond them rises a tree marked with hundreds of
          carved faces, and from one massive branch hangs a house like a lantern-fruit, suspended by a thick rope and a
          counterweighted stone etched in symbols.
    - Hook:
        - Ask what the PCs do to get the Arcanist's attention: call up, climb, study the magic, test the counterweight,
          or try something inventive.
    - Scene role:
        - meaningful player decision
        - important information delivery
        - real potential for things to go wrong
    - Core beats:
        - the house and rope system feel magical, deliberate, and faintly dangerous
        - the Arcanist answers to successful boldness more than perfect manners
        - once invited in, the PCs step into the most concentrated weirdness of the one-shot so far
    - Inside the treehouse
        - potion bottles, runes, organized clutter, contained creatures, and the sensation that every object is exactly
          where it belongs
        - the Arcanist asks about their journey, the person who sent them her way, and how each PC imagines they might
          die
    - Reveal scene
        - the crate contains the capital gate's keystone
        - `Marlowe` recognizes it
        - the King kept the delivery secret because the city is currently unwarded if the keystone is missing
        - the Arcanist declares they must leave for the `Open Vale` immediately to restore the ward
    - Teaching beat:
        - this is a strong place to reinforce that fiction changes stakes before dice do
    - GM note:
        - Play the Arcanist as funny, uncanny, and absolutely serious once the keystone is revealed.

- Scene 6: the `Open Vale` and short rest
    - Read-aloud:
        - The only place in the `Sablewood` without a ceiling of branches waits ahead in a perfect circle of open sky.
          The `Open Vale` feels ancient in a different way than the rest of the forest, as if something here was not
          grown but agreed upon. When the Arcanist steps down, even her usual mischief quiets.
    - Hook:
        - Ask what the PCs do during the hour before the ritual begins: tend wounds, steady nerves, ask questions,
          inspect the site, or prepare together.
    - Scene role:
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
        - meaningful player decision
    - Core beats:
        - the Arcanist explains the Vale as a ritual site and hints that its power carries old cost
        - the party gets a short rest
        - the calm before the climax should feel earned rather than disposable
    - Teaching beats:
        - teach the short rest options clearly
        - explain that `Prepare` together is especially strong for a two-PC party
    - GM note:
        - Let this scene carry a little dread. The players should feel that they are standing on the edge of the real
          problem now.

- Scene 7: the ward renewal
    - Read-aloud:
        - The Arcanist jerks upright as the keystone answers her call. Her glow turns harsh and white-hot, the carriage
          lifts from the ground, and the ritual begins with a shriek that seems to pull a wound open in the forest. All
          around the Vale, the dark answers.
    - Hook:
        - Ask the players where they position themselves around the Arcanist and what kind of protection they are trying
          to offer first.
    - Scene role:
        - real potential for things to go wrong
        - emotional impact
        - meaningful player decision
        - important information delivery
    - Stage-setter:
        - defend the `Whitefire Arcanist` while the ritual countdown reaches `0`
        - ancient skeletons rise nearby
        - forest wraiths close from the dark
    - Possible complications:
        - the Arcanist is struck and the countdown moves backward
        - a `Forest Wraith` makes a PC `Vulnerable`
        - a PC becomes `Untethered`
        - skeleton reinforcements climb from the ground through the Vale itself
        - the party must choose between intercepting enemies and staying close to the Arcanist
    - Teaching beats:
        - reinforce adversary spotlighting
        - use `Fear` openly and clearly so the players see how the engine drives escalation
        - if a PC drops, explain the quickstart's `death move` handling simply and keep the action moving
    - Two-PC adjustment:
        - Start with `3` Ancient Skeletons and `1` Forest Wraith`.
        - Add the fourth skeleton when momentum needs a push.
        - Bring in the second wraith only if the PCs are holding strong or if you want the climax to peak hard near the
          end.
        - Use `Vengeance of the Vale` to control pacing, not to overwhelm the table.
        - Be careful with `Pass-Through`; once in the finale is dramatic, repeated use on a two-PC party is likely too
          punishing.
    - Desired feeling:
        - the party is barely enough, but enough
        - the one-shot closes on earned heroism rather than attrition grind

- Scene 8: epilogue in the treehouse
    - Read-aloud:
        - When the final blow lands, the ritual answers all at once. Light bursts soundlessly from the
          `Whitefire Arcanist`, the last of the hostile magic tears apart, and the `Open Vale` falls still. Later, back
          inside the treehouse, the keystone hangs above the hearth, humming softly as if the city itself were breathing
          through stone.
    - Hook:
        - Ask each PC what they do in the first quiet moment after surviving the Vale.
    - Scene role:
        - emotional impact
        - important information delivery
    - Core beats:
        - the ward is restored
        - `Hush` is safe
        - the Arcanist thanks the PCs and confirms the keystone must remain with her until the magic settles
        - she then offers one final hook: a western `Spire` feels weak, and its `Spirekeeper` may be in danger
    - Closing guidance:
        - If this is the only session, end on the idea that heroes now exist in the `Sablewood` again.
        - If you want a sharper final image, close on the distant sight of a dim tower-light through the trees.

## Secrets and Clues

- The `strixwolf` did not kill the merchant; she found a body and protected a feeding opportunity for her pups.
- Thorn bramble around the cart wheel points to a planned roadside theft rather than random misfortune.
- The merchant's throat was slit, suggesting intelligent attackers before the scavengers arrived.
- The `Thistlefolk` are dangerous in the moment but not automatically monsters; their reputation is broader and crueler
  than the truth.
- `Hush` is protected by a magical ward that the villagers rely on deeply, even if most do not speak of it in technical
  terms.
- The `Whitefire Arcanist` is not just a village eccentric; she is a major protective force holding a dangerous boundary
  in place.
- The sealed crate contains the keystone from the capital city's main gate.
- If the keystone is missing from the capital, its ward is down and the city is more vulnerable than almost anyone
  knows.
- Restoring the keystone's magic is urgent because power this strong draws hostile attention immediately.
- The ritual at the `Open Vale` is not routine for the Arcanist; it costs her.
- Something is also wrong at a western `Spire`, which gives the one-shot its sequel hook without undoing the current
  victory.

## Fantastic Locations

- The Trade Path In The `Sablewood`
    - Rendering: root-broken road, towering ancient trees, strange hybrid-animal calls, and a feeling that the forest is
      measuring travelers
    - Deeper details: the path survives by use more than safety; every bend feels like a place where stories go wrong
- The Overturned Merchant Cart
    - Rendering: split fruit, broken wheels, blood in the dirt, and a watchful `strixwolf` framed by ruin
    - Deeper details: the scene should feel less like a battle map and more like the first page of a fairy tale with
      teeth
- The Ward Marker At `Hush`
    - Rendering: a carved stone pillar, a soft popping sensation in the air, and the sudden return of village sound
    - Deeper details: crossing the boundary should feel like entering borrowed safety inside a dangerous wilderness
- The `Clover Tavern`
    - Rendering: six stories wrapped around a tree trunk, music drifting between floors, shoes hanging overhead, and
      woodsmoke sweetness in the air
    - Deeper details: every level should feel like a slightly different mood of the same communal heart
- The Sunless Farms
    - Rendering: crops topped with glowing blue moss that pulses like breath under moonlight
    - Deeper details: abundance here feels magical but cultivated, cared for, and fragile
- The Whitefire Arcanist's Treehouse
    - Rendering: carved faces in bark, a hanging home, symbol-cut counterweight stone, and flickering yellow-green
      windowlight
    - Deeper details: the house feels precise, alive with craft, and intolerant of careless hands
- The `Open Vale`
    - Rendering: a perfect circle open to the sky, ritual-charged earth, and stillness that feels older than the forest
      around it
    - Deeper details: once the ritual starts, the Vale should feel like a place where the world's seams are visible

## Important NPCs

- `Whitefire Arcanist`
    - Role: ward-keeper of `Hush`, ritual specialist, quest-giver, and strange forest authority
    - Motivation: restore the keystone's ward, protect `Hush`, and keep magical threats contained
    - Roleplay trait: alternates between very slow movements and sudden jerky ones; voice makes emotions obvious even
      when her face does not
    - Use in play: weird, funny, old, competent, and just unsettling enough that the players never treat her like a
      stock wizard
- `Fidget`
    - Role: first local point of contact and living symbol of `Hush` as a safe, lively place
    - Motivation: curiosity about outsiders and enthusiasm for everything happening at once
    - Roleplay trait: never fully still, always climbing, tugging, or peering around something
- `Lausa Standworth`
    - Role: sturdy social anchor and tavern-adjacent local who helps make `Hush` feel grounded
    - Motivation: assess newcomers through their tools, weapons, and manners
    - Roleplay trait: carries a barrel like it weighs nothing and asks blunt questions with total warmth
- `Halython Fives`
    - Role: quiet information source and best route to thoughtful conversation about the road and the `Thistlefolk`
    - Motivation: understand travelers and the dangers pressing around `Hush`
    - Roleplay trait: speaks softly while idly adjusting game pieces, cards, or acorns; often pauses to listen to the
      fox-bat on his shoulder
- The `Strixwolf` Mother
    - Role: first moral test and sign that the forest is dangerous without being simple
    - Motivation: feed, protect her pups, and judge whether the PCs are a threat
    - Roleplay trait: stillness first, sudden violence second; low hoot-growls when warning her pups
- A Surviving `Thistlefolk` if one escapes or surrenders
    - Role: optional bridge that keeps the ambush morally ambiguous
    - Motivation: survive, protect their own, and steal what their people need
    - Roleplay trait: watchful eyes, coiled posture, quick retreat from direct vulnerability

## Monsters or Threats

- `Strixwolf` Mother
    - Role: early pressure, tutorial threat, and moral complication
    - Difficulty: `10`
    - Attack Modifier: `+1`
    - Attack: `Bite` - `Melee` - `1d6+3 phy`
    - Thresholds: `Major 4 | Severe 8`
    - HP: `3`
    - Stress: `3`
    - GM use:
        - best used as a warning creature, not a full fight unless the players choose violence
        - if attacked, the pups flee
        - if mishandled, the mother becomes a real danger but should still feel reactive rather than malicious

- `Thistlefolk Ambusher`
    - Role: skirmisher, distraction, and close-range pressure
    - Difficulty: `13`
    - Attack Modifier: `+1`
    - Attack: `Dagger` - `Melee` - `1d8+5 phy`
    - Thresholds: `6 / 12`
    - HP: `3`
    - Stress: `2`
    - Feature: `Ambush`
        - Reaction: when the Ambusher enters the scene without being spotted first, they can immediately move into
          `Melee` with a target and make an attack against them
        - on a success, they strike with their dagger for `2d4+8 physical damage`
    - Two-PC guidance:
        - use `2` at first
        - hold the third as reinforcement if needed

- `Thistlefolk Thief`
    - Role: leader, mission pressure, and carriage-stealing objective
    - Difficulty: `14`
    - Attack Modifier: `+3`
    - Attack: `Serrated Blade` - `Melee` - `2d4+3 phy`
    - Thresholds: `7 / 14`
    - HP: `4`
    - Stress: `2`
    - Feature: `Back Off`
        - Action: spend a `Fear` to make an attack roll against all targets within `Melee`
        - on a success, targets take `2d6+3 magic damage` and are pushed into `Far` range
    - GM use:
        - this adversary should care more about the carriage and crate than pure slaughter
        - let that goal shape movement and priorities

- `Ancient Skeleton`
    - Role: attrition wave, ritual countdown driver, and controllable pressure source
    - Difficulty: `12`
    - Attack Modifier: `+0`
    - Attack: `Rusted Sword` - `Melee` - `1d6+1 phy`
    - Thresholds: `7 / none`
    - HP: `2`
    - Stress: `1`
    - Feature: `Group Attack`
        - Action: spend a `Fear` to choose a target and spotlight all `Ancient Skeletons` within `Close` range of them
        - those skeletons move into `Melee` and make one shared attack roll
        - on a success, they deal `4 physical damage each`; combine the damage before resolving thresholds
    - GM use:
        - these are ideal pacing tools because they are easy to defeat and advance the countdown
        - use them to make the battlefield feel crowded without turning it into a slog

- `Forest Wraith`
    - Role: elite magical pressure, dread engine, and climax escalator
    - Difficulty: `13`
    - Attack Modifier: `+3`
    - Attack: `Lifedrain` - `Far` - `2d6+8 mag`
    - Thresholds: `Major 7 | Severe 14`
    - HP: `6`
    - Stress: `3`
    - Feature: `Spectral Body`
        - Passive: resistance to physical damage
    - Feature: `Memory Delve`
        - Action: make an attack roll against a `Close` target
        - on a success, the wraith flies into `Melee`, places a hand on the target's cheek, asks for a terrifying
          childhood memory, deals `3d4+9 magic damage`, and makes them `Vulnerable` until their next rest
    - Feature: `Pass-Through`
        - Action: spend a `Fear` and make an attack roll against a `Melee` target
        - on a success, the target becomes `Untethered` and cannot act again until the ritual countdown ticks down,
          clearing the condition
        - if the entire party becomes `Untethered` at once, all mark `2 Hit Points` and their souls return
    - Two-PC guidance:
        - start with `1`
        - add the second only if the party is holding
        - use `Pass-Through` sparingly

- Environment: `The Open Vale`
    - Tier: `1`
    - Type: `Exploration`
    - Feature: `Vengeance of the Vale`
        - Action: spend a `Fear` to summon `2` additional `Ancient Skeletons` from the ground within `Very Close` range
          of a PC
    - GM use:
        - this is the cleanest tool for tuning the climax live
        - if the countdown is dragging, spawn skeletons so the players can win progress by cutting through them
        - if the PCs are already overwhelmed, do not pile this on just because you can

- Final battle structure
    - Ritual countdown starts at `8`
    - It ticks down by `1` each time an adversary is defeated
    - It ticks up by `1` each time the `Whitefire Arcanist` is hit
    - When the countdown reaches `0`, the ritual ends and remaining enemies are dispersed by the Arcanist's power

## Treasure / Rewards

- Immediate rewards
    - safe delivery of the King's package
    - survival and growing trust between the two PCs
    - gratitude and recognition from the `Whitefire Arcanist`
    - the knowledge that they helped keep both `Hush` and the capital from hidden disaster
- Narrative rewards
    - a night of safety, warmth, and belonging after the ritual
    - a sense that the `Sablewood` now knows them as something other than prey
    - the identity of "heroes in the `Sablewood` again"
- Optional tangible rewards if you want a stronger one-shot payoff
    - the Arcanist gifts each PC a small charm marked with warding runes that proves they are welcome in `Hush`
    - a pouch of coin or practical travel goods from the King's promised payment
    - a strange forest trinket from the `Clover Tavern` or `Firstmoss Festival`
- Sequel reward
    - the `Spirekeeper` hook
    - present it as an invitation, not a cliffhanger
    - the one-shot still ends with a completed mission, a restored ward, and a village saved
